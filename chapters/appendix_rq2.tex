\section{Extended mathematical formulation}\label{app:rq2_math}

This section provides the complete set of constraints of the transport sector decarbonization model. Constraints that are also presented with detailed explanations in Section~\ref{sec:rq2_extension} are included here for reference.

\textbf{Objective function:}

The objective function comprises costs for the vehicle stock (${C}^{\mathrm{vehicle\_stock}}$), required fueling (${C}^{\mathrm{fueling}}$), costs for fueling infrastructure (${C}^{\mathrm{infrastructure}}$), intangible costs (${C}^{\mathrm{intangiblecosts}}$), penalty costs (${C}^{\mathrm{penaltycosts}}$), and the VoT-weighted detour cost ($b$). The explicit definitions of each cost component are provided in the following subsections of this appendix. All cost components are discounted to net present value using the discount rate $\delta$:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_obj_fun}
    \begin{split}
        \underset{f,h,h^{+}, h^{-},s,n,b,w,q^{+}, \textrm{budget}^+, \textrm{budget}^-}{minimize} \; z = \sum_{y} \frac{1}{(1+\delta)^{y-y_0}} \Big( {C}^{\mathrm{vehicle\_stock}}_{y} + {C}^{\mathrm{fueling}}_{y} \\ +  {C}^{\mathrm{infrastructure}}_{y} +{C}^{\mathrm{intangiblecosts}}_{y}  +{C}^{\mathrm{penaltycosts}}_{y} + \sum_{i,e,l} b_{yiel} \Big)
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Fueling infrastructure sizing:}

Fueling infrastructure is expanded for each investment period $y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}$ and sized based on a peak-to-annual demand ratio $\gamma_l$. We differentiate between fueling infrastructure independent from the particular trip ($\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{not\;home}}$) and home-based fueling infrastructure ($\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{home}}$):

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_cap_fuel_e}
    \begin{split}
        Q^{\mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{tle} + \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}_y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'tle} \geq \gamma_l\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_e} \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}}  s_{yurvtgle} \quad : \forall y,t,e,l \in \mathcal{L}^{\textrm{not home}} \\
        Q^{\mathrm{charg\_infr, home}}_{urtle}  + \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\textrm{exp}}_y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr, home}}_{y'urtle} \geq \gamma_l\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}}  s_{yurvtgle} \quad : \forall y,u,t,r,e, l \in \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{home}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Demand coverage:}

Decision variable $f$ expresses the number of trips covered by a specific vehicle type and technology-fuel pair of a specific generation. The sum over all vehicle types, technology-fuel pairs and vehicle generations equals the exogenously given travel demand:

\begin{equation} \label{equ:app_demand_cov}
    \begin{split}
        \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_v} \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}} f_{yurvtg} = F_{yur} \quad :  \forall y, u, r
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

The right side of the equation expresses the coverage of the demand by different vehicle types and drivetrain technologies of different generations.

\textbf{Fleet sizing:}

Based on the trips covered by a vehicle type of a technology, the vehicle stock is sized as follows:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_fleet_size_demand}
    \begin{split}
         h_{yurvtg} \geq \frac{L_{r}}{W_{vtg} L^{\mathrm{annual}}_{vtg}} f_{yurvtg} \quad :\forall y,u,r,v,t,g
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Vehicle stock balance with vintage tracking:}

The vehicle stock of each generation $g$ is tracked using the carried-over stock variable $h^{\mathrm{exist}}$. The stock balance for each vintage is:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_fleet_size_change_1}
    \begin{split}
         h_{yurvtg} = h^{\mathrm{exist}}_{yurvtg} + h^{+}_{yurvtg} - h^{-}_{yurvtg} \quad :\forall y,u,r,v,t,\; g \leq y
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

The carried-over stock $h^{\mathrm{exist}}$ represents the vehicles of generation $g$ that are inherited from the previous year. Its definition depends on the relationship between the model year $y$, the vehicle generation $g$, the initial year $y_0$, and the vehicle lifetime $\Lambda_{vtg}$:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_h_exist}
    h^{\mathrm{exist}}_{yurvtg} = \begin{cases}
        H^{\mathrm{init}}_{urvtg} & \text{if } y = y_0,\; g < y_0,\; y_0 - g \leq \Lambda_{vtg} \\[0.5ex]
        h_{(y-1)urvtg} & \text{if } y > y_0,\; g < y,\; y - g \leq \Lambda_{vtg} \\[0.5ex]
        0 & \text{if } g = y \;\text{(new generation)} \\[0.5ex]
        0 & \text{if } y - g > \Lambda_{vtg} \;\text{(lifetime exceeded)}
    \end{cases}
\end{flalign}

where $H^{\mathrm{init}}_{urvtg}$ is the exogenously given initial vehicle stock for pre-existing generations. For the initial year, pre-existing generations are initialized from data; for subsequent years, the stock is carried over from the previous year. Vehicles of generation $g = y$ (newly produced) have no carry-over. Vehicles that have exceeded their maximum lifetime $\Lambda_{vtg}$ are set to zero.

New vehicle purchases are restricted to the current generation:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_h_plus_gen}
    \begin{split}
         h^{+}_{yurvtg} = 0 \quad :\forall y, u, r, v, t, \; g \neq y
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

Equation~\ref{equ:app_fleet_size_demand} expresses the sizing of the vehicle stock via a vehicle's annual range $L^{\mathrm{annual}}$ and load factor $W$. The stock balance (Equation~\ref{equ:app_fleet_size_change_1}) with the vintage-tracked carry-over (Equation~\ref{equ:app_h_exist}) governs the aging of the vehicle fleet. Vehicles exit the stock when their maximum lifetime is reached, and new purchases (Equation~\ref{equ:app_h_plus_gen}) are only possible for the current generation.

\textbf{Fueling demand:}

Fueling demand is derived from the number of trips $f$ and via the specific demand $D^{spec}$ of the vehicle:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:fuel_demand}
    \begin{split}
        \sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}_{t}} \sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}_{rl}} s_{yurvtgle} \geq \frac{D^{\mathrm{spec}}_{vtg} \cdot L_{r}}{W_{vtg}} f_{yurvtg} \quad : \forall y,u,r,v,t,g
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Number of fueling vehicles:}

$n$ indicates the number of fueling vehicles and is derived from the fueled energy, accounting for the usable state of charge $\mathrm{SOC}^{+,\mathrm{charging}}$:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_n_fueling}
    \begin{split}
        n_{yurlge} \geq \sum_{v,t} \frac{1}{\mathrm{SOC}^{+,\mathrm{charging}} \cdot \textrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{tank}}_{vtg}}s_{yurvtgle} \quad : \forall y,u,r,g,e, l \in \mathcal{L}^{vt}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Maximum capacity constraints:}

The geographic allocation of the fueling demand is given by the summation over all geographic elements that are traveled by the vehicle. The summation over all geographic locations along the travel path and different types of fueling infrastructures $l$ ensures that the allocation of fueled energy along the path is an endogenous decision. The expansion of fueling capacities is also limited by maximum values:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_max_cap}
    \begin{split}
        Q^{\mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{tle} + \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}_y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'tle} \leq \hat{Q}^{\mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{tle} \quad : \forall y, t, l, e \\
        \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_u} \left( Q^{\mathrm{charg\_infr, home}}_{urtle}  + \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\textrm{exp}}_y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr, home}}_{y'urtle} \right)\leq \hat{Q}^{\mathrm{charg\_infr,home}}_{utle} : \forall y, u, t, l, e
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Intangible costs and level of service:}

The intangible costs represent non-monetary costs monetized via the consumer-group-dependent value of time (VoT). In the MILP extension, the detour cost component is captured separately by the variable $b_{yiel}$ (see below), so the intangible costs reduce to the VoT-weighted travel time:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_intangible}
    \begin{split}
        {C}^{\mathrm{intangiblecosts, total}} =  \sum_{y,u,r,v,t,g} \mathrm{VoT}_{u} \cdot \mathrm{LoS}^{f}_{yrvt} \cdot f_{yurvtg}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

The level of service (LoS) is defined as the total travel time:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_los}
    \begin{split}
        \mathrm{LoS}^f_{yrvt} = \frac{L_r}{\mathrm{speed}_{vt}} + \mathrm{fueling\_time}_{yrvt} \quad :\forall y,r,v,t
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Monetary budget constraints:}

Monetary budgets constrain expenditures for vehicles and home charging infrastructure per consumer group and purchase cycle $\mathcal{S}^u_j$:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_lower_constraint}
    \begin{split}
        \sum_{y \in \mathcal{S}^u_j} \left( \sum_{vtg}C^{\mathrm{CAPEX}}_{yvtg} \cdot h^{+}_{yurvtg} + \sum_{tl}\sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\textrm{exp}}_{y}} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr, home}}_{yurtle} \right) \\ \leq \beta \cdot \tau_u \cdot \mathrm{Budget}_{u} \cdot \sum_{y \in \mathcal{S}^u_j}\sum_{vtg} f_{yurvtg}    + \sum_{y \in \mathcal{S}^u_j} \mathrm{budget}^{+}_{yur}  \quad : \forall u,r,j
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_upper_constraint}
    \begin{split}
       \sum_{y \in \mathcal{S}^u_j} \left( \sum_{vtg}C^{\mathrm{CAPEX}}_{yvtg} \cdot h^{+}_{yurvtg} + \sum_{tl}\sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\textrm{exp}}_{y}} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr, home}}_{yurtle} \right) \\ \geq \beta \cdot \tau_u \cdot \mathrm{Budget}_{u} \cdot \sum_{y \in \mathcal{S}^u_j}\sum_{vtg} f_{yurvtg}    - \sum_{y \in \mathcal{S}^u_j} \mathrm{budget}^{-}_{yur}  \quad : \forall u,r,j
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

where $\mathrm{budget}^{+}_{yur}$ and $\mathrm{budget}^{-}_{yur}$ are decision variables for budget overrun and underrun, penalized in the objective function. The penalty cost term in the objective function is defined as:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_penalty_costs}
    \begin{split}
        {C}^{\mathrm{penaltycosts}}_{y} = M^{\mathrm{penalty}} \cdot \sum_{u,r} \left( \mathrm{budget}^{+}_{yur} + \mathrm{budget}^{-}_{yur} \right)
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

where $M^{\mathrm{penalty}}$ is a large penalty coefficient that ensures budget constraints are nearly binding while preserving model feasibility.

\textbf{MILP detour time extension:}

The total installed charging capacity is decomposed into a discrete component tied to detour-reduction levels and a continuous residual. Let the total installed capacity at location $e$ for charging type $l$ up to year $y$ be defined as the sum of the initial capacity $Q^{\mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{tle}$ and the cumulative expansion $\sum_{y'} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'tle}$. This total capacity is linked to a discrete capacity level selected by the binary variable $w_{yiel}$:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_cap_decomp}
    \begin{split}
        Q^{\mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{tle} + \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}_y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'tle} = \sum_i w_{yiel} \cdot cap^{\mathrm{lb}}_{iel} + \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}_y} q^{\Delta+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'tle} \quad : \forall y,t,e,l \in \mathcal{L}^{\textrm{not home}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

The left-hand side is the total installed capacity (initial plus cumulative expansion). The right-hand side decomposes it into a discrete component $cap^{\mathrm{lb}}_{iel}$, selected by the binary variable $w_{yiel}$, and a continuous residual $q^{\Delta+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}$ that captures the capacity above the discrete threshold. The indicator constraint ensures that when level $i$ is active, the total capacity lies within the associated bounds:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_indicator}
    \begin{split}
        w_{yiel} = 1 \implies cap^{\mathrm{lb}}_{iel} \leq Q^{\mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{tle} + \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}_y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'tle} \leq cap^{\mathrm{ub}}_{iel} \quad : \forall y,i,e,l \in \mathcal{L}^{\textrm{not home}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

Exactly one detour-reduction level is selected per location, year, and charging type:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_sum_w}
    \begin{split}
        \sum_i w_{yiel} = 1 \quad : \forall y, e, l  \in \mathcal{L}^{\textrm{not home}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{VoT-weighted detour cost:}

The detour cost captures the additional travel time needed to reach charging infrastructure, monetized via the consumer-specific VoT. Based on an initial detour time $B^{\mathrm{init}}_{el}$ and its reduction $\alpha_{iel}$ at the active capacity level $[cap^{\mathrm{lb}}_{iel},\; cap^{\mathrm{ub}}_{iel}]$, the decision variable $b_{yiel}$ is defined via an indicator constraint:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_vot_dt}
    \begin{split}
        w_{yiel} = 1 \implies b_{yiel} \geq B^{\mathrm{init}}_{el} \cdot (1-\alpha_{iel}) \cdot \sum_{u,r,g} \mathrm{VoT}_u \cdot n_{yurlge} \quad : \forall y, i, e, l \in \mathcal{L}^{\textrm{not home}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

The variable $b_{yiel}$ enters the objective function directly and represents the total monetized detour cost at location $e$ for charging type $l$ in year $y$. The implementation uses indicator constraints natively supported by the solver. For solvers without indicator support, the equivalent big-M reformulation is:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_bigM_lb}
    \begin{split}
        b_{yiel} \geq B^{\mathrm{init}}_{el} \cdot (1-\alpha_{iel}) \cdot \sum_{u,r,g} \mathrm{VoT}_u \cdot n_{yurlge} - M \cdot (1 - w_{yiel}) \quad : \forall y, i, e, l \in \mathcal{L}^{\textrm{not home}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_bigM_ub}
    \begin{split}
        b_{yiel} \leq M \cdot w_{yiel} \quad : \forall y, i, e, l \in \mathcal{L}^{\textrm{not home}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

where $M$ is a sufficiently large constant. Equation~\ref{equ:app_bigM_lb} activates the lower bound on $b_{yiel}$ when $w_{yiel} = 1$ and relaxes it otherwise. Equation~\ref{equ:app_bigM_ub} forces $b_{yiel} = 0$ when $w_{yiel} = 0$.

The following additional constraints complete the formulation.

\textbf{Technology stock transition rate:}

The year-over-year change in the vehicle stock of a drivetrain technology $t$ is bounded to prevent unrealistically rapid technology transitions:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_tech_shift}
    \begin{split}
        \left| \sum_{v} \sum_g h_{yurvtg} - \sum_{v} \sum_g h_{(y-1)urvtg} \right| \leq \alpha^h_t \sum_{v,t',g} h_{yurvt'g} + \beta^h_t \sum_{v} \sum_g h_{(y-1)urvtg} \\ :\forall y \in \mathcal{Y}\setminus\{y_0\}, u, r, t
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

where $\alpha^h_t$ and $\beta^h_t$ are the maximum rate-of-change parameters relative to the total vehicle stock and the previous stock of the respective technology.

\textbf{Vehicle-type stock transition rate:}

The year-over-year change in the stock of an individual vehicle type $v$ and technology $t$ combination is similarly bounded:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_vtype_shift}
    \begin{split}
        \left| \sum_g h_{yurvtg} - \sum_g h_{(y-1)urvtg} \right| \leq \alpha^h \sum_{v',t',g} h_{yurv't'g} + \beta^h \sum_g h_{(y-1)urvtg} \\ :\forall y \in \mathcal{Y}\setminus\{y_0\}, u, r, v, t
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Market share constraints:}

For vehicle technologies with exogenously defined market shares $\mu_{yvt}$, the share of newly purchased vehicles is fixed:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_market_share}
    \begin{split}
        \sum_{u,r,g} h^+_{yurvtg} = \mu_{yvt} \cdot \sum_{u,r,v',t',g} h^+_{yurv't'g} \quad :\forall (y, v, t) \in \mathcal{MS}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

where $\mathcal{MS}$ denotes the set of year--technology--vehicle type combinations with defined market shares.

\textbf{Trip purpose ratio:}

The ratio between commuting and private trip vehicle stocks is fixed per geographic location to maintain the observed trip-purpose distribution:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_trip_ratio}
    \begin{split}
        \sigma^{\mathrm{commuting}}_e \cdot \sum_{\substack{r \in \mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{private}}_e \\ v,t}} h_{yurvtg} = \sigma^{\mathrm{private}}_e \cdot \sum_{\substack{r \in \mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{commuting}}_e \\ v,t}} h_{yurvtg} \quad :\forall y, e, g
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

where $\sigma^{\mathrm{commuting}}_e$ and $\sigma^{\mathrm{private}}_e$ are the exogenously given trip shares for commuting and private purposes at location $e$, and $\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{commuting}}_e$ and $\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{private}}_e$ denote the respective subsets of OD pairs originating at $e$.

\textbf{BEV fleet growth:}

The total BEV fleet within each consumer group must not decrease over time:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_bev_growth}
    \begin{split}
        \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_u} \sum_{v} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{BEV}}} \sum_g h_{yurvtg} \geq \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_u} \sum_{v} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{BEV}}} \sum_g h_{(y-1)urvtg} \\ :\forall y \in \mathcal{Y}\setminus\{y_0\}, u
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Minimum home charging share:}

For consumer groups with a defined minimum home charging share $\sigma^{\mathrm{home}}_{yu}$, the energy charged at home must be at least a specified fraction of the total electricity consumed by that consumer group:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_min_home}
    \begin{split}
        \sum_{\substack{r \in \mathcal{R}_u,\, v,\, t,\, g \\ e = \mathrm{origin}(r),\; l \in \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{home}}}} s_{yurvtgle} \geq \sigma^{\mathrm{home}}_{yu} \cdot \sum_{\substack{r \in \mathcal{R}_u,\, v,\, t,\, g \\ e,\, l}} s_{yurvtgle} \quad :\forall y, u
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Forced fast charging for long-distance trips:}

Trips whose path length exceeds the usable driving range of the vehicle are restricted to fast charging infrastructure only:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_forced_fast}
    \begin{split}
        n_{yurlge} = 0 \quad :\forall y, u, r, l \notin \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fast}}, g, e \quad \text{if } L_r > \frac{\mathrm{SOC}^{+,\mathrm{driving}} \cdot \mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{tank}}_{vtg}}{D^{\mathrm{spec}}_{vtg}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Slow and fast charging expansion ratio:}

The cumulative expansion of public slow charging infrastructure is bounded relative to the expansion of public fast charging at each location:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_slow_fast}
    \begin{split}
        \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}_y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'(l=\mathrm{slow})e} \leq 2 \cdot \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}_y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'(l=\mathrm{fast})e} \quad :\forall y \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}, e
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

\textbf{Infrastructure expansion rate limits:}

The expansion of public charging infrastructure is bounded by initial-period capacity limits and subsequent growth rate constraints:

\begin{flalign} \label{equ:app_infr_expansion}
    \begin{split}
        &q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y_0 le} \leq \overline{q}^{\mathrm{init}} \quad :\forall e, l \in \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{pub}} \\
        &q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{yle} - q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{(y-\Delta y)le} \leq \rho \cdot \Delta y \cdot \left(Q^{\mathrm{charg\_infr, init}}_{le} + \sum_{y' < y} q^{+, \mathrm{charg\_infr}}_{y'le}\right) \\
        &\qquad :\forall y \in \mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{exp}}\setminus\{y_0\}, e, l \in \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{pub}}
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

where $\overline{q}^{\mathrm{init}}$ is the maximum new infrastructure investment in the initial period and $\rho$ is the maximum growth rate per investment period.

\section{Pre-processing of travel data}\label{app:rq2_travel_data}

\subsection{Trip lengths by purpose}

Two groups for the environmental setting of a trip are introduced: \textit{urban} and \textit{rural}. The segregation is based on the population distribution between cities with population size greater than 100{,}000 people of the Basque Country, together with the respective mode share between urban and rural areas (20\%/50\% of trips by private car). This, together with the information on the trip purpose, serves as information to approximate the path length.

These assumptions are drawn from a survey on mobility for Austria \citep{tomschy2016osterreich}, as this survey contains detailed information on average trip lengths by purpose for all NUTS-2 regions in Austria. Specifically, for urban areas, trip lengths recorded for Vienna are used, and for rural areas, the trip lengths recorded for suburban areas are used due to the high population density in the Basque Country.

\subsection{Clustering of trips to vehicles}

The clustering of trips to vehicles introduces a constraint linking commuting and private vehicle stocks. To consider the technology decision vehicle-based rather than trip-based, the share of technology must follow a ratio related to the application of the vehicles. Trips are grouped by income class and origin to $M$ route clusters, resulting in sets of routes $\mathcal{R}_{m}$. Based on empirical values on the relative frequency of trips per purpose, the vehicle stocks are related to each other with the following constraint:
\begin{flalign}
    \begin{split}
         \zeta_1 h_{yur(p=\textrm{commuting})vtg} \leq  \zeta_2 h_{yur(p=\textrm{private})vtg} \quad :\forall y, u, v, t, g,  r \in \mathcal{R}^{m},  m = 1, 2, \dots, M
    \end{split}
\end{flalign}

Table~\ref{tab:ratio_zeta_rq2} displays the parameter values by region.

\section{Discretization of the detour-fueling function}\label{app:rq2_discretization}

To introduce the relationship between installed charging infrastructure capacity and the detouring time to the closest available charging station into the mixed-integer linear program, the continuous detour-fueling function is discretized into piecewise steps. This discretization balances the trade-off between the loss of information due to discretization and the increase in model complexity (number of binary variables) with finer resolution. Figure~\ref{fig:dtf_sampling_rq2} illustrates the sampling of the fueling detour time functions used in the MILP formulation.

\section{Computational details}\label{app:rq2_computational}

The model is implemented in Julia using JuMP~1.9 \citep{Lubin2023} and solved with Gurobi~12.0 \citep{gurobi}. Simulations are performed on an AMD Ryzen Threadripper 7980X (64 cores, 3.20~GHz base clock, 5.2~GHz boost) with 512~GB RAM. The model comprises approximately 32 million continuous variables and 189 binary variables. After presolve, the problem is reduced to approximately 2 million variables. A single scenario solves in approximately 6.5 hours, terminating at an MIP gap below 0.005\%.

The source code and input data are publicly available at: \url{https://github.com/antoniamgolab/iDesignRES_transcompmodel}.

\section{Sensitivity analysis: value of time}\label{app:rq2_vot}

\subsection{Quantification of value of time}\label{app:rq2_vot_quantification}

The value of time (VoT) represents the monetary value that consumers assign to the time spent detouring to reach a charging station. The Victoria Transport Policy Institute recommends valuing personal travel, including commuting, at approximately 50\% of the hourly wage rate \citep{litman2023travel}. For transit under comfortable conditions, recommended values range from 25--35\% of wage, while crowded conditions can justify values up to 100\%. Similarly, a recent World Bank meta-analysis of VoT studies in low- and middle-income countries found that VoT corresponds to approximately 50\% of after-tax wages \citep{wardman2023meta}.

Table~\ref{tab:danish_vot_rq2} shows that the Danish VoT values used in the original source \citep{TATTINI2018265} fall within these empirically supported ranges, with VoT/wage ratios spanning from 13\% (Q1) to 63\% (Q5).
